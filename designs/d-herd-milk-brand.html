Herd Milk: identity board

Direction D, round two. A bright, fresh drink brand for milk and milkshakes from a self-serve vending hut, made from milk splashes, drips, pours and crowns. It is based on the idea that the Antrobus Herd might sell its own milk; we do not know yet that it does. Herd Milk is a working name. Prepared 23 November 2026. See it on a homepage.

The logo

A cobalt milk splat with drips, and a heavy, friendly wordmark whose i is dotted with a pink drop. Stacked inside the splat for bottles and signs; side by side for the website and the hut.

Lettering

Fill. Pay. Go.

Bricolage Grotesque, a free typeface with chunky, slightly quirky letters at large sizes that stay clear and calm when small. Extra bold for the wordmark and headlines, semi bold for labels, regular for reading.

Headlines are short and said out loud: "Fresh from the herd. Fill your own."

The splash system

Five shapes, all cut from the same milk. They move: the crown rises when the page loads, drips stretch as you reach them, the bottle fills. They hold still for anyone who has asked their device for less motion.

The crown

A splash rising as a bottle lands. Used once, big, per page or pack.

The splat

The logo's ground, and a frame for photos of the herd.

The drip edge

Where one colour meets another, milk drips over the join.

The drop

Dots the i in Milk. Bullets, map pins and small marks.

The pour

A single clean stream: shows filling, and leads the eye down the page.

Name ideas

Herd Milk stays as the working name. Five alternatives, each with the reason it might suit.

Working name

Herd Milk

The working name. Short, says what it is and ties straight back to the Antrobus Herd.

Morris Farm Milk

Names the real farm, so the provenance is in the name itself.

Black and White

The colours of a Holstein, and a nod to keeping it simple: milk, no fuss.

The Pinta Hut

Old British slang for a pint of milk, with the hut you fill it at.

Lane Milk

From the herd that walks down the lane past the farm; easy to say and remember.

Antrobus Pour

Keeps the village name the herd already carries, with the act of filling up.
