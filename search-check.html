We looked at your website the way Google and a first-time visitor do. Here is what we found, in plain English, with no jargon.

6 of 10

Your site passes 6 of the 10 basic checks. Google can find you and the groundwork underneath is sound. Four gaps affect how you look once you are found, and there is one thing about the site itself we would put right first.

Worth fixing first

Your website says almost nothing about your cattle.

Across all six pages there are only about a hundred words. The British Blues page has a heading and no words at all, and nothing anywhere says what the herd is known for, what you breed for or whether anything is available.

Under your Holstein photos are three labels left over from the original template: "Lessons", "Guide, single day" and "Guide, multi day adventure". A visitor could easily think the farm sells riding lessons or days out.

The contact box shows empty spaces for a mobile number, an email and a website, and there is no email address or form anywhere, so the only way to reach you is the landline. A new site would tell the story of the herd and both breeds in your own words, and make getting in touch one tap.

Four smaller things to fix

All four are quick to put right.

To fix

Shared links show no photograph

When someone shares your site with another breeder, or in a farming group, no picture goes with the link. You have lovely photographs of your cattle; none of them is used.

A new site would attach a photograph of the herd and one clear line to every share.

To fix

Your main heading does not say what you are

Every page opens with the same main heading, "Antrobus Herd". It is the first thing a search engine reads, and it never says cattle, British Blues, Holsteins or Cheshire.

A new site would open with a heading that says it plainly: pedigree British Blue and Holstein cattle at Antrobus, near Northwich.

To fix

Your photographs are labelled with camera numbers

Each picture can carry a short written description, used by image searches and read aloud to visitors who cannot see the screen. Yours are labelled with camera file names such as "IMG00127-20110806-0804.jpg".

A new site would describe every photograph in plain words: a British Blue cow with her calf, the herd coming down the lane.

To fix

Google is not handed your details

Websites can give Google a small fact sheet: what the business is, where it is and the number to ring. Yours tells Google only that the site is called "antrobusherd".

A new site would include that fact sheet, with Morris Farm, your phone number and both sides of the business: the herd and the contracting.

To fix

A summary file for AI tools

Your site already has an AI summary file, made automatically by Wix. But its title is just "antrobusherd" and it says nothing about the herd, the breeds or the contracting; it only lists technical settings.

A summary file for AI tools needs a few honest sentences: who you are, the two breeds, the contracting work and how to get in touch.

Nobody can make an AI recommend a business, and anyone who offers to is selling nothing. What a website can do is let these tools reach it, read it and get your facts right.
